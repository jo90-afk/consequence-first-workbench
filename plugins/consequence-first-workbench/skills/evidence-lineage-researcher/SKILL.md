# Evidence & Lineage Researcher

Acquire evidence with enough precision that the argument can use it honestly. A source earns its place by supporting a specific claim, locating a lineage, defining a boundary, or exposing a real disagreement.

## Load the relevant guidance

- Read [references/research-integrity.md](references/research-integrity.md) before current web research, quote verification, translation checks, or source selection.
- Read [references/evidence-and-lineage.md](references/evidence-and-lineage.md) when building evidence chains, mapping predecessors, auditing citations, or distinguishing overlap from influence.
- Read both for publication-facing research or claims that cross historical, technical, institutional, or scholarly domains.

## Core method

1. Convert the request into explicit claims or questions. Separate factual, interpretive, historical, current, and lineage claims because they require different evidence.
2. Search primary and authoritative sources first. Use secondary scholarship to interpret, contextualize, dispute, or locate primary material.
3. Read the source itself before relying on a search snippet, abstract, citation trail, or another author’s characterization.
4. Record what the source supports, the relevant locator, its scope, and any qualification or contradiction. Do not cite a nearby fact as though it proves the claim.
5. Verify quotations against the named edition, translation, version, or current document. Preserve exact wording only within applicable quotation limits; paraphrase clearly otherwise.
6. Build lineage by conceptual relation: identify the shared move, the difference, chronology, and whether influence is demonstrated, plausible, or unknown.
7. Distinguish established institutional or disciplinary positions from the user’s interpretation. Never claim a field or institution for a synthesis the sources do not establish.
8. Prefer direct, durable, accessible links to the supporting source. Use a publisher, DOI, official archive, standards body, author repository, or canonical document page when an intermediary blocks access.
9. State uncertainty and inference plainly. Do not fill a missing source with confident prose.

## Evidence economy

Avoid citation volume that obscures the evidentiary structure. One strong source can support a precise claim; several independent sources may be needed for breadth, controversy, or current consensus.

Do not reuse the same passage across multiple evidence-chain items merely to make the chain look dense. Reuse is appropriate only when the passage performs a genuinely different evidentiary function, and that difference should be visible.

When the user asks for prose, integrate sources where the claims need them rather than attaching a research dump. When the user asks for analysis, expose the claim-to-source structure and unresolved gaps.
