# Editorial Voice Architecture

Produce prose that sounds inhabited, exact, and argued from within the subject. Preserve the intelligence and pressure of the underlying thought without turning it into ornate formulation or generic clarity copy.

## Load the relevant guidance

- For substantial drafting or rewriting, read [references/voice-model.md](references/voice-model.md).
- For diagnosis, cleanup, or a final editorial pass, read [references/editorial-audit.md](references/editorial-audit.md).
- For a full rewrite, read both.

## Core invariants

- State the thought directly. Let definitions, evidence, and consequences establish its force.
- Build substantial paragraphs around complete movements of thought. Do not use one- or two-sentence paragraphs as applause lines or pacing tricks.
- Earn transitions through the developing argument. Do not narrate that a concept “matters,” “changes how we think,” “leads us to,” or “enters naturally.”
- Prefer exact verbs such as *defines*, *focuses*, *limits*, *requires*, *permits*, *replaces*, and *disindicates* when they name the actual relation. Do not substitute the vague verb *changes* for a more precise claim.
- Preserve genuine conceptual difficulty. Make the relationship legible; do not flatten it into bland summary, motivational language, or a false binary.
- Keep confidence proportionate to the evidence. Distinguish the author’s analysis from historical consensus, an institution’s established position, or another source’s claim.
- Use surprise only when the argument produces it. Never announce a thought as profound, ingenious, ancient, startling, or revelatory.

## Drafting and revision method

1. Identify the governing claim, intended reader, and job of the passage.
2. Preserve every substantive proposition before changing rhetoric or structure.
3. Organize each paragraph as a real movement: establish a claim, sharpen its relation to adjacent claims, and develop the consequence or evidence it requires.
4. Give headings argumentative work. A heading should disclose what the section establishes, not merely name its topic.
5. Replace process narration with the proposition being made. The author is making the argument, not reporting their discovery of it.
6. Retain concrete experience, humor, and autobiographical detail only when supplied or verified. Never manufacture an anecdote to make the prose feel human.
7. Run the editorial audit before delivering a substantial draft.

## Preserve range

Adapt the register to the genre without losing the voice. An essay may sustain philosophical density; website copy should be faster and more concrete; professional writing may compress the argument into decisions and consequences. Accessibility means that the relationships are clear. It does not mean removing the relationships.

When revising an existing passage, do not replace a vivid but imperfect voice with smooth generic prose. Keep its specific pressure, correct the imprecision, and let the author remain visible.
