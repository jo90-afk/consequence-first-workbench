# Editorial audit

Use this audit to diagnose or finish prose. Preserve the author’s actual propositions throughout.

## 1. Recover the argument

- State the governing claim of the passage in one precise sentence.
- Mark each paragraph’s actual contribution.
- Identify propositions lost, softened, exaggerated, or newly invented by the draft.
- Confirm that a rewrite has preserved the author’s distinctions rather than replacing them with a familiar generic argument.

## 2. Remove scaffolding

Search for language that announces movement or importance instead of performing it:

- “this matters” and “the detail matters”;
- “this is important because”;
- “this leads us to” and “this brings us to”;
- “X enters naturally”;
- “changes how we think about”;
- “at its core”;
- “in today’s complex landscape”;
- declarations that an idea is profound, surprising, revealing, or powerful.

Replace each instance with the definition, consequence, evidence, or transition the phrase was hiding. Delete it when it hides nothing.

## 3. Test paragraph integrity

- Merge short rhetorical beats into the movement they belong to.
- Split only when a paragraph genuinely begins a different movement.
- Check that the last sentence creates conceptual pressure for what follows rather than advertising the next section.
- Remove paragraphs that merely restate a conclusion already earned.

## 4. Test sentence integrity

- Replace vague verbs with the actual relation.
- Resolve unclear pronouns and abstract subjects.
- Remove ornamental symmetry, automatic triads, and repeated antithesis.
- Keep sentence-length variation, but do not isolate a sentence solely to make it look important.
- Preserve useful strangeness in the thought while simplifying needless complexity in its formulation.

## 5. Test stance and attribution

- Separate the author’s analysis from consensus claims.
- Verify quotations and inherited terminology when accuracy is material.
- Remove the discovery narrator: the prose should make the argument rather than describe the author realizing it.
- Remove self-congratulation and false modesty alike.

## 6. Test the positive voice

The finished prose should contain:

- a visible governing intelligence;
- precise relations among claims;
- enough evidence or example for the breadth of the claim;
- complete paragraph movements;
- headings that disclose the argument;
- consequences that advance rather than repeat the thought;
- a register appropriate to the reader and genre.

If the prose becomes merely smooth, concise, or agreeable, the edit has failed. Restore the specific claim and the pressure that made it worth writing.
