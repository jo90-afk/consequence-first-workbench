# Editorial integrity

## Separate editorial passes

Choose the pass the user requested and avoid smuggling in another kind of revision.

### Substantive pass

Evaluate thesis, claim dependency, missing premises, contradictions, scope, evidence, and consequences. Changes may require renewed approval.

### Structural pass

Reorder sections, combine repetition, develop transitions through argument, and align headings with the explicit claims they carry.

### Voice pass

Restore the author’s positive prose model, remove scaffolding and false profundity, strengthen exact verbs, and preserve conceptual density.

### Source pass

Verify quotations, translations, names, dates, lineage, citations, and attribution boundaries against inspected sources.

### Line and copy pass

Correct syntax, diction, spelling, transliteration, punctuation, consistency, and typographic defects without altering the argument silently.

### Format pass

Apply styles, numbering, contents, headers, page breaks, notes, bibliography, and publication layout while preserving text.

State when a requested change necessarily crosses pass boundaries.

## Canonical terminology

Maintain current values for:

- book title and subtitle;
- chapter and section titles;
- names, transliterations, and technical terms;
- defined concepts and their exact relations;
- institutional versus authorial attribution;
- translation and quotation standards;
- labels used by companion sites or reader interfaces.

When one value changes, search active manuscript surfaces and dependent metadata. Preserve old values only in historical discussion, quotations, source titles, or revision history.

## Quotations and citations

- Verify direct quotations against the cited edition or version.
- Apply the project-specified translation consistently.
- Preserve section, page, line, paragraph, timestamp, or other source locators as appropriate.
- Keep paraphrase outside quotation marks.
- Confirm that each citation supports the adjacent claim at its actual breadth.
- Distinguish a missing citation from a missing argument.
- Avoid carrying forward a spelling or citation defect merely because it appeared in an approved earlier draft; correct it through an explicit copy or source pass.

## Front matter

Treat the introduction, lineage, method, terminology, scope and attribution notes, and reading guidance as part of the argument rather than administrative packaging. Revisit front matter when later chapters establish distinctions it currently misstates or claims too broadly.

Do not let front matter claim consensus for the manuscript’s synthesis. Acknowledge genuine predecessors by the conceptual work they share and the points where the manuscript differs.

## Document integrity

After editing a formatted manuscript:

- confirm the canonical file identity;
- compare expected and actual section order;
- verify the inserted or revised text;
- update numbering, contents, cross-references, notes, and bibliography as required;
- render affected pages and inspect headings, page breaks, spacing, tables, notes, and overflow;
- ensure no tracked placeholder, temporary marker, duplicated heading, or orphaned content survives;
- retain accessibility and document properties required by the project.

Text extraction alone cannot verify page layout. Rendering alone cannot prove the correct text was incorporated. Use both forms of evidence when layout is material.

## Publication checkpoint

At a publication checkpoint, run the broad integrity gate the project requires: full terminology audit, quotation and citation verification, contents and cross-reference update, formatting review, metadata, export, and version declaration. Do not pay this full cost after every sentence-level iteration.
